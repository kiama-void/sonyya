I don't necessarily think distillation of American AI models by Chinese labs, followed by releasing those models as open source, is inherently unfair.

We are already moving towards a world where a handful of companies, particularly American ones, are accumulating enormous power over AI infrastructure and intelligence.

There are also significant environmental costs associated with this race: GPU consumption, energy requirements, water usage and the infrastructure needed to train increasingly large models. Yet, somehow, the narrative around this concentration of resources and power is becoming increasingly normalised.

If the environmental costs of building this intelligence are borne collectively, why should its benefits remain concentrated among a few companies and countries?

The computational resources, research and intelligence being developed should eventually create opportunities for ordinary people, independent researchers, smaller labs and developing economies.

In that context, distillation and open-weight releases by Chinese labs seem like one of the more successful approaches we've seen towards redistributing access to AI capabilities.

I see this less as a question of whether one company deserves credit for another's work, and more as a question of who gets to benefit from technological progress.
